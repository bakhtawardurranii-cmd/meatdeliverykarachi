About Beef Nihari Cut

Nihari depends almost entirely on using the right cut of beef — shank and connective-tissue-rich pieces that break down slowly over hours of cooking, releasing collagen that gives nihari its signature thick, glossy gravy. Regular boneless beef simply doesn't have enough connective tissue to produce this texture, which is why we select and cut beef specifically for nihari rather than substituting a general-purpose cut.

Cut & Portion Information

Beef nihari cut is selected from shank and adjoining connective-tissue-rich portions of the animal, cut into pieces sized for a long, slow braise. A 1kg order is typically enough for a nihari serving 5–6 people, since nihari is usually served with generous gravy alongside a smaller amount of meat per portion.

Bone-In Shank for a Rich Gravy

Beef nihari cut includes bone-in shank pieces, as the bone marrow and surrounding connective tissue are exactly what give nihari its rich, gelatinous gravy after a long cook. This is not a cut we recommend making boneless, since removing the bone would defeat the purpose of using it for nihari in the first place.

Minimum Order & Preparation Options

Minimum order for beef nihari cut is 1kg. We cut this specifically as shank and connective-tissue-rich pieces suited to slow cooking — it isn't interchangeable with our boneless beef or beef BBQ cuts, so please order this cut by name when making nihari.

Recommended Dishes

This cut is made specifically for beef nihari, and can also be used for other slow-braised, gravy-heavy beef dishes such as beef paya-style preparations where a rich, thick gravy is the goal.

Cooking Recommendations

Nihari needs a long, slow cook — traditionally 4–6 hours on low heat, or 60–90 minutes in a pressure cooker — to fully break down the connective tissue into gravy. Don't rush this cut on high heat; the whole point of using shank is the slow transformation of collagen into a thick, glossy sauce, which simply doesn't happen with a quick cook.

Storage & Freshness

Refrigerate immediately after delivery and use within 1–2 days, or freeze for up to two months in an airtight bag. Thaw fully in the refrigerator before starting your slow cook, as partially frozen shank pieces cook unevenly.

Beef Nihari Cut — Frequently Asked Questions

Why can't I just use boneless beef for nihari?

Boneless beef lacks the connective tissue and marrow found in shank, so it won't produce the thick, gelatinous gravy nihari is known for after a long cook.

How long does beef nihari cut need to cook?

Traditionally 4–6 hours on low heat, or 60–90 minutes in a pressure cooker, to fully break down the connective tissue.

Is beef nihari cut bone-in?

Yes, it includes bone-in shank pieces, as the bone marrow contributes to the gravy's richness.

How many people does 1kg of beef nihari cut serve?

Typically 5–6 people, since nihari is served with generous gravy per portion.
