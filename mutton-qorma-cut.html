About Mutton Qorma Cut

Mutton qorma cut is jointed for slow-cooked, curry-style dishes where the mutton simmers gently in a yogurt or nut-based gravy until deeply tender. Compared to a karahi cut, qorma cut often includes a slightly higher proportion of pieces with a little more fat and connective tissue, since the long, gentle simmer of a qorma is well suited to breaking these down into a smooth, rich gravy.

Cut & Portion Information

Mutton qorma cut is jointed into medium bone-in pieces, generally 8–10 pieces per kilogram. A 1kg order typically serves 4–5 people as part of a curry meal, since qorma is usually served alongside rice or naan rather than as the sole dish.

Bone-In for a Rich, Slow-Cooked Gravy

Mutton qorma cut is bone-in, as the long, gentle simmer typical of qorma preparation benefits from the bone marrow and connective tissue breaking down slowly into the gravy, in the same way it does for karahi, but over an even longer cook time.

Minimum Order & Preparation Options

Minimum order for mutton qorma cut is 1kg. Pieces are jointed to a medium size suited to slow simmering. If you're making a lighter, quicker qorma, we can cut the pieces slightly smaller — just mention this when ordering.

Recommended Dishes

This cut is made for mutton qorma, and also works well in other slow-cooked, gravy-based mutton curries such as korma-style dishes served at family gatherings and special occasions.

Cooking Recommendations

Marinate mutton qorma cut in yogurt for at least an hour before cooking to help tenderize it, then simmer gently on low-medium heat for 45–60 minutes, or pressure cook for 20–25 minutes, until the meat is fork-tender. Qorma gravies are typically built slowly with browned onions and ground nuts or poppy seeds — avoid rushing this base, as it's central to the dish's flavor.

Storage & Freshness

Refrigerate immediately after delivery and use within 1–2 days, or freeze for up to two months in an airtight bag. Thaw fully in the refrigerator before marinating and cooking.

Mutton Qorma Cut — Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between mutton qorma cut and mutton karahi cut?

Both are bone-in and similarly sized, but qorma cut is chosen for pieces that suit a longer, gentler simmer in a yogurt or nut-based gravy rather than a quick high-heat karahi.

How long should mutton qorma cut be marinated?

At least an hour in yogurt is recommended before cooking, to help tenderize the meat.

How long does mutton qorma cut take to cook?

Around 45–60 minutes of gentle simmering, or 20–25 minutes in a pressure cooker.

How many people does 1kg of mutton qorma cut serve?

Typically 4–5 people as part of a curry meal with rice or naan.
