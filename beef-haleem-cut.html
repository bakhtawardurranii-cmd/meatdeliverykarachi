About Beef Haleem Cut

Haleem depends on the meat shredding completely into the lentil and wheat base after hours of slow cooking, which only happens reliably if the beef is cut into small, even pieces to begin with. Large or unevenly sized chunks take longer to break down and can leave stringy, under-shredded pieces in the final dish. Our beef haleem cut is prepared specifically to shred well, saving you the extra step of cutting it down yourself.

Cut & Portion Information

Beef haleem cut is cut into small, uniform pieces from lean beef portions, supplied by weight. A 1kg order is generally enough for a haleem batch serving 8–10 people, since haleem is typically bulked out significantly with lentils and wheat.

Boneless, Cut for Shredding

Beef haleem cut is boneless, cut small and even specifically so it shreds cleanly once fully cooked. This is different from our beef nihari cut, which deliberately keeps bone-in shank pieces for a thick gravy — haleem instead needs the meat itself to break apart into fine shreds.

Minimum Order & Preparation Options

Minimum order for beef haleem cut is 1kg. Pieces are cut small and uniform as standard for haleem. If you're making a different slow-cooked shredded-meat dish, this cut also works, but let us know your dish so we can confirm the sizing suits it.

Recommended Dishes

This cut is made specifically for beef haleem, and also works for other dishes where fully shredded, well-cooked beef is the goal, such as some pulao and biryani preparations that use shredded meat as a topping.

Cooking Recommendations

Pressure cook or slow cook beef haleem cut with lentils and wheat/barley for several hours until the meat is completely tender and can be shredded with a spoon or hand blender. Traditional haleem preparation often involves cooking for 4+ hours, then beating or blending the mixture to fully break down the meat and grains into a smooth, thick consistency.

Storage & Freshness

Refrigerate immediately after delivery and use within 1–2 days, or freeze for up to two months. Thaw fully in the refrigerator before your slow cook so the pieces cook and shred evenly.

Beef Haleem Cut — Frequently Asked Questions

Why is beef haleem cut different from boneless beef?

It's cut into smaller, more even pieces specifically so the meat shreds cleanly during haleem's long cook, rather than staying in chunks.

Is beef haleem cut bone-in?

No, it's boneless, since haleem needs the meat itself to shred, not bone marrow for gravy.

How many people does 1kg of beef haleem cut serve?

Around 8–10 people, since haleem is bulked out significantly with lentils and wheat.

How long does haleem need to cook?

Traditionally 4 or more hours until the meat is tender enough to shred and blend into the lentil-wheat base.
