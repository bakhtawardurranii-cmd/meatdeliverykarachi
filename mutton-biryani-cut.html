About Mutton Biryani Cut

Mutton biryani cut is jointed into smaller, more evenly sized pieces than a karahi or qorma cut, so the meat finishes cooking in roughly the same time as the rice layered on top of it during dum. Mutton is naturally slower to cook than chicken, so getting this cut size right is especially important for biryani — pieces that are too large risk undercooked meat once the rice is done.

Cut & Portion Information

Mutton biryani cut is jointed into smaller, evenly sized bone-in pieces, generally 10–12 pieces per kilogram. A 1kg order is typically enough for a biryani serving 5–6 people, given biryani is generally served with rice making up the bulk of each portion.

Bone-In for Traditional Mutton Biryani

Mutton biryani cut is bone-in by default, matching how mutton biryani is traditionally prepared — the bone contributes flavor to the masala the rice absorbs during the dum (steaming) process.

Minimum Order & Preparation Options

Minimum order for mutton biryani cut is 1kg. Pieces are jointed to a smaller, biryani-appropriate size as standard, distinct from our karahi and qorma cuts. If you're preparing a boneless-style biryani, ask us and we can prepare boneless mutton pieces instead.

Recommended Dishes

This cut is made specifically for mutton biryani and mutton pulao, and any layered rice dish where the meat and rice need to finish cooking together.

Cooking Recommendations

Because mutton takes longer to cook than chicken, mutton biryani typically needs a partial pre-cook of the meat (often in the masala/yakhni base) before layering with partially cooked rice for the final dum — going straight from raw meat to dum without pre-cooking usually leaves the meat undercooked. Marinate in yogurt and biryani masala for at least an hour beforehand, and allow enough time in the yakhni stage for the mutton to become tender before assembling the final layers.

Storage & Freshness

Refrigerate immediately after delivery and use within 1–2 days for the best result, or freeze for up to two months. Thaw fully in the refrigerator before marinating and cooking.

Mutton Biryani Cut — Frequently Asked Questions

Why is mutton biryani cut smaller than mutton karahi cut?

Smaller, even pieces help the meat finish cooking in roughly the same time as the rice during the dum process, since mutton is slower to cook than chicken.

Do I need to pre-cook mutton before making biryani?

Yes, mutton generally needs a partial pre-cook in the masala/yakhni stage before layering with rice, unlike chicken biryani which can sometimes go in with less pre-cooking.

Is mutton biryani cut bone-in?

Yes, by default, which is traditional for Karachi-style mutton biryani.

How many people does 1kg of mutton biryani cut serve?

Typically 5–6 people, since rice makes up the bulk of most biryani portions.
